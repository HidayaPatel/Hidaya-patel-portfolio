Regression Analysis — Starbucks Sugar Content

Statistical Analysis  ·  R  ·  Data Science

Overview

This statistical analysis investigates the nutritional composition of Starbucks beverages to identify which characteristics most strongly predict sugar content. Using a comprehensive dataset of Starbucks menu items, the project applies linear regression modeling with rigorous diagnostics to uncover the key drivers of sugar levels across the product range.

The analysis was conducted entirely in R, leveraging the tidyverse ecosystem for data wrangling and ggplot2 for rich, publication-quality visualizations. Special attention was paid to model assumptions — linearity, homoscedasticity, normality of residuals, and absence of influential outliers — to ensure the validity of statistical inferences.

Key Features

  • Nutritional Factor Analysis — Systematic investigation of calories, fat, carbohydrates, fiber, protein, and sodium as predictors of sugar content.
  • Regression Diagnostics — Residual plots, Q-Q plots, Cook's Distance, and VIF multicollinearity checks to validate every model assumption.
  • Visualization of Predictors — Scatter plots with regression lines, partial regression plots, and coefficient forest plots built with ggplot2.
  • Significance Testing — F-tests, t-tests on individual coefficients, and confidence intervals to identify statistically meaningful predictors.
  • Model Comparison — Stepwise selection and AIC/BIC criteria used to arrive at a parsimonious final model from the full predictor set.

Tech Stack

R Linear Regression ggplot2 Statistical Modeling tidyverse dplyr R Markdown

Results

The final linear regression model explained a substantial proportion of variance in sugar content across Starbucks beverages. Carbohydrate content emerged as the strongest single predictor, followed by calorie count and serving size. Fiber content showed a significant negative association with sugar, aligning with nutritional theory. Model diagnostics confirmed that assumptions were sufficiently met for valid inference, and the results provide actionable insight for consumers monitoring dietary sugar intake.
