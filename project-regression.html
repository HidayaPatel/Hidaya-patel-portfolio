What drives the sugar in your Starbucks?

I tested which nutrition facts best predict the sugar in a drink. Three of them do nearly all the work.

Guess a drink's sugar

Move the sliders and the fitted model estimates the sugar. The ranges match the drinks in the data.

sugar = 0.237 × calories − 2.68 × fat − 2.54 × fibre − 3.31

Read it this way: at the same calories, a drink with more fat or fibre has less room for sugar.

Calories lead, fat follows oddly

On its own, fat goes up slightly with sugar. Hold calories steady and the relationship flips to negative, which is why a one-variable chart would mislead you.

Correlation between pairs of variables

1 is a perfect straight-line relationship, 0 is none.

Dropping half the predictors cost almost nothing

I started with six and removed caffeine, saturated fat and trans fat. The simpler model explains nearly as much.

I checked residuals, normality and leverage. Three drinks stood out as outliers, and I kept them because nothing suggested they were errors.

What I'd do differently

Sugar contains calories, so part of this strong fit is arithmetic rather than discovery. My report also ended on a version without an intercept, whose R² of 0.987 can't be compared with the others, so I show the standard model here.
