Can you predict a subway delay?

I took every incident Toronto's subway logged from January 2023 to September 2026, joined it with hourly weather, and built a model of delay risk by station and hour. Then I tested it on a year it had never seen.

Why: mostly people, not trains

Two thirds of lost time comes from passenger, security and miscellaneous incidents, such as ill customers, disorderly passengers and people at track level. Train equipment is only 8%.

Cleaning: 1,687 spellings, 75 stations

Station is typed by hand and cut off at 22 characters. I matched each entry to a real station by its longest known prefix and mapped renamed stations to their current name, which placed 96% of incidents.

Model: does it hold up on 2026?

The question: at this station, in this hour, will there be a delay of 5 minutes or more? Trained on 1.5 million station-hours from 2023 to 2025, tested on 376,740 from January to September 2026. Only 0.9% of them had a delay, so I scored ranking (AUC), not accuracy.

Honest limits: risk, not prophecy

An AUC near 0.65 is modest, and that is the finding. Most delays are close to random at one station in one hour. Station carries nearly all the signal, then hour of day. A simple lookup table gets most of the way, and machine learning adds a small, real gain.

How weather changes the delay rate

Delays of 5+ minutes per hour, compared with dry hours between -10 and 25°C.

Heavy snow looks dramatic but covers just 52 hours, so treat it as rough. Once station and hour are known, weather adds almost nothing to the model.
