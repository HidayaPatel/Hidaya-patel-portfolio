Can technical signals beat the market?

My capstone: ten years of Apple prices, 24 technical features, three models and a trading backtest. The answer was no, and showing why is the point.

The backtest: holding still won

The strategy buys when the model predicts "up" and holds cash otherwise, over 541 days the model never trained on. It returned +15.6% after costs. Simply holding Apple returned +58.6%.

Growth of $1, October 2023 to December 2025

Strategy includes a 0.1% cost each time it enters or exits.

The dashboard's original figure is -14.3%. It charged the 0.1% cost on all 365 days the strategy held the stock. Charging it on the 65 entries and exits gives +15.6%. The conclusion is the same either way: it loses to buy and hold.

The more complex the model, the more it fooled itself

XGBoost looked best in training at 72% and fell to 47.7% on new data. Logistic regression fell the least and had the best F1 score, so I chose it. None of the three beat always guessing "up".

What went in

Daily Apple prices and volume from Yahoo Finance, turned into 24 features and split by time, so the model only ever learned from the past.

FamilyFeatures
TrendSimple and exponential moving averages, MACD, price relative to the 50-day average
MomentumRSI, stochastic oscillator, 5 and 10-day momentum
VolatilityATR, Bollinger Band width, 10 and 30-day volatility
Recent returnsToday's return and lags of 1, 2, 3 and 5 days
VolumeVolume relative to its 20-day average

What I took from it

A baseline turns a score into a finding: 50.5% only means something next to 54.3%. And a backtest is only as good as its cost assumptions, so check those before trusting the curve.
